2 · Supervised Learning: the Recipe of Deep Learning

A neural network is a function with many adjustable parameters, assembled from simple pieces. Each neuron forms a weighted sum of its inputs and passes it through a non-linear activation function, and a layer of neurons is a matrix product followed by these activations. The universal approximation theorem guarantees that such functions can approximate any continuous function on a bounded domain, given enough neurons. Training finds the parameters. We define a loss that measures the error of the network on the data and lower it by gradient descent, with the gradients computed by backpropagation, the chain rule organised so that a computer applies it automatically.

The first complete example is the recognition of handwritten digits in the MNIST data set (Modified National Institute of Standards and Technology). We first train a network with a single output and the mean squared error, which treats the ten digits as points on a line. We then train a network with ten outputs and the categorical cross-entropy, which treats them as ten categories. The confusion matrices of the two networks show how the choice of the loss shapes what the network learns.

Lab 004: Automatic differentiation

A computer can differentiate any program built from elementary operations, to the accuracy of floating-point arithmetic. We show how automatic differentiation does it and why it is the basis of training.

Lab 005: The N-link pendulum with SymPy and automatic differentiation

We derive the equations of motion of a pendulum with N links twice, symbolically with SymPy and numerically by automatic differentiation of the Lagrangian, and compare the two.

Lab 006: Universal approximation and the choice of minimiser

Networks of growing width fit a target function more closely, as the universal approximation theorem promises. How close a trained network actually gets depends on the optimiser, and we compare several.

Lab 007: Building and training neural networks

We assemble a complete training loop in PyTorch, with data loading, the forward pass, the loss, backpropagation and the parameter update.

Lab 008: Analysing a neural network

After training, a network can be inspected. We read off its architecture and parameter counts and examine what its layers compute.

Lab 009: MNIST, regression versus cross-entropy

We reproduce the two formulations of digit classification from the slides, a single output with the mean squared error and ten outputs with the categorical cross-entropy, together with their confusion matrices.
